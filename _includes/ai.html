Where I Think AI Is Going · And Where I Fit

A working position, not a forecast: four shifts I am building against — and the part of each one that thirty years of data and delivery work actually makes easier.

01

The harness becomes the product

agents

The 2026 conversation has moved past the prompt. What production teams argue about now is the harness around the model — tool contracts, retries, budgets, permissions, the trace you replay after a bad run. Once you see it that way it is an unglamorous distributed-systems problem: idempotency, back-pressure, exactly-once, audit. That is the work I have been doing since Sybase and MQ-era banking systems.

  • Tool / MCP contracts designed like APIs, not scripts
  • Failure semantics: retry, compensate, escalate to a human
  • Cost and latency budgets treated as SLOs
02

Context beats cleverness

retrieval

Most disappointing AI features are not model failures, they are retrieval failures. The durable differentiator is the boring middle: clean schemas, lineage, chunking that respects document structure, permissions enforced at query time, and knowing what to leave out. My PDF Table Extractor already had to reconstruct a table's structure from an unstructured page — the same instinct, one architecture later.

  • Semantic + lexical + structured hybrid retrieval
  • Row-level security carried into the vector store
  • Freshness and provenance on every retrieved fact
03

Evaluation is the new regression suite

evals

You cannot ship what you cannot measure. The teams getting value out of agents are the ones who built a dataset, a grader and a gate before they built the demo — golden cases in CI, scored on every change, with a human in the loop only where judgement is genuinely required. This is the part most teams skip, and it is where database discipline pays for itself.

  • Versioned eval sets that fail the build, not the user
  • Traces and feedback loops as first-class data
  • Cost, latency and quality measured together
04

Regulated verticals are the open field

opportunity

Agent tooling is crowded; wealth, lending, insurance and health are not — and they are exactly where an agent has to be auditable, explainable and boring. Twenty years inside WealthTech and digital lending is not a disadvantage in that market, it is the entry ticket. The value is not a smarter model; it is a defensible process the regulator accepts.

  • Human-in-the-loop where advice is regulated
  • Deterministic audit trail for every automated decision
  • Small, owned models where data cannot leave the building
